oma-solorpg · a solo virtual tabletop for Omarchy

Your agent is the game master

◆

You play a tabletop RPG alone, tonight, with no prep. Claude Code or Codex runs the game from behind a book of prose, and you answer it in words. A small engine owns the dice, the rules and every word said at the table, and the agent has to go through it for all of them.

install it tonight watch the trailer 45 s

Plays Ironsworn out of the box, its free rules shipped with credit, and Dragonbane, built from your own copy of the rules.

an omarchy-shell plugin · standard-library Python 3.11 · no API keys · no network · MIT

The Book

where the story is read and told

◆

A coding agent in a terminal prints its tool calls, permission prompts and JSON between the lines of your story. The Book takes the story out of the terminal. The agent runs headless behind it, its narration streams in as it writes, and what you read is prose.

  • Your hero's skills speak up. Entering a scene rolls each one quietly, and only a success reaches the margin: SPOT HIDDEN [5] SUCCESS. You never learn what you missed.
  • A torch burns down with game time, and dark places are dark.
  • The codex keeps everyone you've met, with a face each, and blanks out what they want, fear and hide until you find out.
  • Omens from hidden countdowns reach you as omens, never as numbers.
  • When your hero dies, the Book closes on a gravestone with your last words, and Home keeps them in the Hall of the Fallen.

The dice are code

    
the agent narrates, the engine decides

    
◆

  


  
Questions nobody rolls for go down a fixed ladder: the adventure's text first, then a skill check, then the oracle, which takes its odds from what the campaign tracks. The agent never picks the answer. Every change is an event in events.jsonl, and the state is rebuilt from it at any time.


  

    

      
how a turn works

      
    
        
  1. youwrite to the GM at the bottom of the Book
    2. 
        
  2. agentpicks one intent from what solo scene offers
    3. 
        
  3. enginesolo check sneaking: the dice and the rules, in code
    4. 
        
  4. agentreads the scene and proposes the consequence
    5. 
        
  5. enginesolo commit '{"facts": …}': validated, logged, clocks advance
    6. 
        
  6. agentnarrates what was committed
    7. 
      

      
Fights, dying and advancement are rules the engine runs: initiative cards, weapon damage through armor, death rolls at zero HP, and skills marked by Dragons and Demons and rolled up at the end of a session.

playing alone, by the book

      
With Dragonbane's own solo rules built into your pack (they're in Alone in Deepfall Breach, in the core set), the engine runs the game the way the book means it to be played alone.

      
    
        
  • Your hero starts with one more heroic ability. Army of One deals you two initiative cards in a fight; Sole Survivor lets you push a roll for willpower instead of a condition.
    • 
        
  • Questions a GM would answer go to the book's fortune chart: yes or no, how many, how big, how strong, how fine, or how someone reacts. Open questions get words to read.
    • 
        
  • Threats loom on a counter that climbs with every delay, and come for you when it fills.
    • 
        
  • You can search an area and scavenge a nest. Foes the adventure never wrote fight as minions or bosses.
    • 
        
  • At a rest you tend your own wounds. At zero HP you can rally yourself, or try to save your own life.
    • 
      

      
Without a solo rules book the oracle is the engine's own, a likelihood oracle that brings surprises: random events tied to the people and threads in play, and scenes that turn out altered, as often as a chaos factor says.

Bring your books

    
rules and adventures, read from PDFs you own

    
◆

  


  
oma-solorpg ships Dragonbane's words and none of its rules. make dragonbane reads the books it knows from your PDFs, by their bookmarks and layout, with no agent. For a book it doesn't know, your agent reads it a chapter at a time and writes a pack the engine can run, every number citing the page it came from. The book never goes through a chat upload, however big it is, and nothing built from it lands in the repository.


  

    

      
what make rules does

      
    
        
  1. solo extract turns the PDF into pages and chapters of text, with the running heads taken out and the printed page numbers read off the footers.
    2. 
        
  2. Any page that holds a roll table is also saved as a picture, because text extraction scrambles columns and the agent can look at the page instead. A page with no text on it is flagged as a scan.
    3. 
        
  3. solo inventory starts a checklist: an item for every section and table, every page cited.
    4. 
        
  4. Your agent works through it with the solo-rules-import skill: attributes, creation tables, weapons and armor, fear and mishap tables, a bestiary, the price lists, a rules page per topic.
    5. 
        
  5. solo audit fails on anything unaccounted for, either way, and on any table result, die, monster stat or price that isn't on the page it cites.
    6. 
      

      
make rules BOOK=… SOLO_BOOK=… runs the mechanical steps and then opens your agent with the skill and a prompt. The pack lands in ~/Games/solo/systems, laid over the names the repository ships.

adventures, too

      
make adventure ID=my-adventure PDF=~/Books/dragonbane-adventures.pdf
make adventure ID=my-adventure FOUNDRY=~/Exports/my-adventure
make check-adventure ID=my-adventure

      
From a PDF, or from a Foundry VTT export (journals, actors and roll tables). The solo-import skill adds what the text only implies: factions, clocks, gates, branches, people with profiles and voices. It also says how to turn a published adventure's table rules into solo ones: a real-time limit becomes game time, pre-made replacement heroes wait for your death, a tournament's score is kept.

      
solo outline shows the whole adventure on one page for review (spoilers), and solo validate points out scenes nobody can reach. The adventure then shows up on the New adventure screen.

A long campaign

    
the campaign remembers, so the GM doesn't have to

    
◆

  


  
A campaign can run for many sessions, and the GM may start any of them knowing nothing. The agent's session can be lost, compacted or replaced at any time, and the campaign holds the memory for it.


  
    
    
  • What your hero does comes back. The GM writes down what will follow from a deed (the guard who took the bribe will brag, the cultist who fled will warn his master, the debt at the inn falls due in two days) and the engine tells it when the moment comes. You only see the world react.
    • 
    
  • People remember what you did to them, and word spreads to their kin. Someone the GM made up keeps their name, face, voice and wants from one session to the next.
    • 
    
  • A place you return to is the place you left: the forced gate still hangs open, and the GM is told how long you were away.
    • 
    
  • Before it brings anything back, the GM searches everything ever said at the table (solo recall), so a name it gave you three sessions ago stays the same name.
    • 
    
  • Close the Book whenever you like. It opens again exactly where you stopped, word for word.
    • 
  


  

    

      
generate a campaign

      
When no book holds the story you want, pitch one. Give your agent a premise, a tone and a length, and it writes the campaign as a pack: a hub to come back to, missions as paths of waypoints, factions that want things, clocks and threats, people with faces, voices and secrets.

      
make campaign ID=salt-and-ash TONE=grim MISSIONS=4 \
  PREMISE="a smuggler's coast where the dead keep the lighthouses"

      
Your system's own tables and the oracle roll the parts, so the dice have a say before you start. Missions after the first are written from what your hero actually did: the consequences they left open, the people who remember them. Then solo validate, solo outline and solo campaign check check it like any imported adventure, every roll used or explained, and it waits on the New adventure screen.

    


    

      
heroes carry on

      
A hero who lives through an adventure brings their story into the next one: how it ended, what was left open, who will remember them, and what you told the GM about them. A lost sister, an oath.

      
solo new red-tusk --character ~/Games/solo/campaigns/sinking-tower-ragna

      
And one who doesn't makes it into the Hall of the Fallen, with your last words.

dragonbane, from your books

      
sudo pacman -S python-pymupdf
make dragonbane BOOKS=~/Books/Dragonbane
make check

      
Dragonbane plays from your own PDFs. make dragonbane reads a folder of them and builds the core rules, the Book of Magic, the card decks, the solo booklet, The Sinking Tower and the Adventure Book's campaign into private packs in ~/Games/solo, in about twenty seconds and with no agent. make check audits them against the books. Then New adventure has The Red Tusk Hall and your books' adventures.

      
PyMuPDF reads the PDFs; the README says how to install it outside Omarchy. A book no importer knows goes to your agent: make rules BOOK=….
